Systems Thinking Definition

In my own words systems thinking means in understanding and solving complex issues on how system parts operate individually and how they work together. This overall means that instead of focusing on a single aspect the systems thinking approach allows for taking all parts of a systems together which can allow one to comprehend on how each of them work to make a system operate.

Differs From Traditional (Linear) Ways of Thinking

Systems thinking differs from traditional linear ways of thinking due to there being differences on them having a different means of how issues are broken down to further understand on how everything works. Systems thinking differentiates from traditional linear thinking because traditional linear thinking instead involves a much more simplified method on focusing on a single aspect of a system. Meaning that traditional thinking involves in solving problems in a much simpler way to solve problems within a system instead of focusing on the entire system. This can prove beneficial for a business’s decision process due to it making it much easier. This is beneficial because for businesses it allows them to scope a decision down in a narrow approach which makes it much easier to find cause and effect factors and emphasizes on them (Lee, 2023). However, the traditional linear approach has its flaws that can make it less effective than the systems thinking approach that is used in certain situations. For example that approach has its flaws that makes it weaker than a systems thinking approach that is due to its narrow approach which it ignores a complex system and focuses only on an aspect of a system only (Miker, n.d.). Despite this where systems thinking is more beneficial to use is that it can help one to be able to come up with many and various solutions to a problem more so than the traditional linear way of thinking. Due to a traditional linear way of thinking only having a narrow focus where systems thinking is better is that it can benefit businesses to be able to assess unintentional impacts with great accuracy as well as navigating any risks or opportunities (Lee, 2023). Overall, businesses that use the systems thinking approach will experience greater benefits through many and various solutions than simply using the traditional linear approach which would limit their solutions to a problem or the scope of a system.

Why a Model-Based Approach is Applicable

The model-based approach is applicable to the design of complex digital systems because it helps to tie a complex digital system all together, helps with cybersecurity which ultimately helps in reducing any development risks. Through the usage of a model-based approach it helps the design of complex digital systems to be at an advantage. First, a model-based approach helps to bring everything together that are model, systems thinking and systems engineering to bring into the design of complex digital systems (Shevchenko, 2024). Through the implementation of the model-based approach encompassing all those three factors helps in reducing risks. This is because the model-based approach helps to rather mitigate security risks in the early stages of the system-development process thus making it much secure by design instead of adding those security mitigations later on the process (Shevchenko, 2024). This overall contributes in helping analysts to be able to find and mitigate any potential risks or threats much earlier which can help to prevent any future negative impacts on a system.

Second Benefit of Using the Model-Based Approach

The second benefit of using the model-based approach is that if its implanted into the design of complex digital systems it helps in keeping track of those systems. This is beneficial for businesses that may have much more complex systems that may have difficulty in effectively managing and tracking them. Through everything being connected it can help businesses to efficiently problem solve due to the model-based approach allowing businesses to trace elements of a solution (Shevchenko, 2024). Overall, through the usage of the model-based approach it helps business in making them effective to contribute in effectively and efficiently tracking and solving problems much easier in complex systems.

Systems Development Life Cycle Defined

The definition of system development life cycle is that it encompasses seven modules or steps that all contribute in successfully launching software. All those seven modules ensure that the progress of a software’s development goes smoothly and that each phase is able to transition well into the next phase until everything is complete. The systems development life cycle can also have a different meaning that can also describe a system (Holt, 2023).

Key Characteristics

The seven key characteristics of the system development life cycle is that it contains the following phases being planning, analysis, design, coding, testing, deployment and maintenance.

Phase One

The first key characteristic of the system development life cycle out of seven is the planning phase. The planning phase involves on employees or people discussing and planning the purpose of the software being created. To elaborate more the planning phase can also include having to suggest input from other people such as stakeholders, business analysts, line-of-business managers and internal and external customers (Jackson et al., 2025). Through these contributions it helps in improving the development plan of the software that is going to be created and released.

Phase Two

The second key characteristic of the system development life cycle out of seven is the analysis phase. This phase involves the team that is developing the software which is analyzing the information of the software’s project. This phase contributes in helping the software project’s development team because it involves analyzing the project alongside collecting user needs, researching the market, testing porotypes, and resources (Jackson et al., 2025). Overall, this phase teaches the development team about meeting expectations based on quality through analyzing information.

Phase Three

The third phase of the system development life cycle involves designing the software’s architecture. This refers to the project’s development team putting into great consideration into designing the software’s core features. It requires the software engineers in choosing the best options in designing the software and considering the apps, services and as well as the security features of the software that results in a software design document being created (Jackson et al., 2025). This will ensure that the software’s design features and security features are effectively prepared for the following phase that involves coding.

Phase Four

After, the design phase of the system development life cycle concludes it leads to the coding phase. The coding phase involves using the previous phases plans to coding them according on the results of the previous phases that was setup. The results also known as the documents from the previous phases help software developers to guide them in choosing the correct programming language (Jackson et al., 2025). Overall, this helps to contribute to helping software developers in being focused solely on relying on the documents from the previous phases to effectively write the code.

Phase Five

Following the coding phase the testing phase begins but it only occurs when the software is ready to be tested. During the testing phase the development team that is developing the project has the purpose of ironing out the software by improving on any flaws or patching any bugs, vulnerabilities and anything that can damage the final product. The quality assurance team usually performs the testing phrase upon the software to further on ensure that the software is functioning that helps to meet any user or business requirements (Jackson et al., 2025). Overall, after the testing of the software is successful to be fully operational with no flaws or vulnerabilities the software will be ready to be deployed which after this phase the deployment phase follows.

Phase Six

After the successful testing of the software the prepared software becomes ready to be deployed to the production environment where it can be accessed. This phase does not just only mean to just releasing the software for being accessible. Instead, the development phase’s main purpose is for users being able to understand the about to be released software that can have small disruption to user experience or workflows (Jackson et al., 2025). Overall, after this there is still much to be done even after the software is released to the public which it has to have maintenance.

Phase Seven

Finally, after the deployment phase the maintenance phase occurs there has to be continuous updates to the software to ensure the software’s longevity. Through this it can ensure that a software continues in making sure that any bugs or vulnerabilities are patched out or that a software is further optimized (Jackson et al., 2025). Overall, despite a product being released to keep the software to continue being operational and have longevity several flaws and additions have to be implemented to keep users retained.

Advantages and Drawbacks of Linear, Iterative and Incremental Life Cycle Models

Advantages of Linear Life Cycle Models

The usage of linear life cycle models is that it can provide many advantages to a business that uses this framework. The linear life cycle model provides many advantages that can help the structure of a business based on control and predictability. The examples of benefits that come with using linear cycle management are clear workflow, defined milestones, better predictability, and other benefits (Linear Project Management Indepth Overview & 14 key benefits, 2024). The great advantages that can come with using the linear life cycle model is that it can help a business to have more control of their business and have better predictability of certain aspects of the business.

Drawbacks of Linear Life Cycle Models

Despite the benefits that using a linear life cycle model brings it can also cause problems for the business that is using the linear life cycle model. As a whole through the usage of the linear life cycle model it can dampen a business’s decisions or can impact their financial stance. The several examples of there being disadvantages to the linear life cycle models are that they can limit flexibility, impact delays, replanning costs and lower suitability for uncertainty (Linear Project Management Indepth Overview & 14 key benefits, 2024). Overall despite the great benefits that the linear life cycle models brings it can also bring its disadvantages that can negatively affect a business’s efficiency.

Advantages of Iterative Life Cycle Models

An advantage of businesses using the iterative life cycle models is that it can greatly help certain aspects of software projects. Examples of having the iterative life cycle model is that it provides flexibility, early feedback, reduces risks, improves software quality and other examples that can help to improve the software’s development (Talreja, 2025). Overall, these benefits can greatly help a business project involving software for its development phase to go much more smoothly and the results of the software are up to meeting expectations.

Drawbacks of Iterative Life Cycle Models

However, the benefits of the iterative life cycle models can greatly benefit a software’s development, but it can also bring its fair share of problems. Such problems that iterative life cycle models can bring to businesses is that it lessens predictability, budget, needs constant communication/collaboration, needs experienced team members and other flaws that can dampen a software’s development (Talreja, 2025). This overall can cause problems that can have negative impacts to the software’s development cycle but even with these drawbacks a business can still be able to succeed in order to develop the software.

Advantages of Incremental Life Cycle Models

The advantages of a business using incremental life cycle models is that it can help on delivering parts of software’s system a step by step process which ensures that the development phase goes smoothly. The examples of advantages of using incremental life cycle models is that it can bring early feedback, adaption to changes, and it can minimize initial investment (Incremental Model of Software Development Life Cycle, n.d.). Overall, through these advantages it helps businesses to have a smooth process in developing the needed software that meets the expectations of the business.

Drawbacks of Incremental Life Cycle Models

Despite the benefits of the incremental life cycle model it also brings with it drawbacks that can negatively impact the software project. Several examples of the drawbacks of using the incremental life cycle model is that it can make it complex in managing requirements, there is also problems regarding integrating the different parts of a system together, it also needs great planning with communication, and it needs to depend on clients for any feedback (Incremental Model of Software Development Life Cycle, n.d.). Overall, despite the great benefits of using the incremental model it can have consequences by making the development cycle of the software much more difficult and complex.

Reflection in Terms of a System’s Life Cycle Importance

Reflection on Design

When reflecting on why thinking in terms of a system’s life cycle is important for the design, sustainability, and evolution of digital systems is that it ensures that each part of a system functions. First, when it comes to the thought of the design of digital systems based on the system’s life cycle is that it is important to find any problems that of most importance to then solve. In order to find any problems within a system it is best practice to look at the whole system as a whole because this will help them to be able to make conclusions on the positives and negatives of software (Autodesk Support, 2018).

Reflection on Sustainability

When it comes to sustainability it is important to consider if a system isn’t releasing any harmful emissions that can harm the environment. To have a product to be sustainable it has to be done during the designing phase of a system. This is because it can help in identifying any parts of a product’s lifecycle that might have negative impacts and it can help to determine on what can be done to minimize those impacts (Autodesk Support, 2018). Overall, through reflecting on sustainability it can help a development team to determine the harmful emissions that might come from a product which then it can help the development team to then lessening those impacts.

Reflection on Evolution

In terms of reflecting on evolution when it comes to a system there must be reflection on it because a system’s life cycle must continue to evolve to meet new standards that are set. This is because most products or systems can’t often stay the same due to new technology being introduced which system developers must utilize to make improvements. Such improvements to the system can be in regards in having better designs or meeting sustainability standards better that can make a business more efficient.
