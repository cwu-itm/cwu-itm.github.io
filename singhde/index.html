Understanding Systems Thinking and Model-Based Systems Engineering

Introduction

Technology has become a major part of almost everything we do today. We use computers, phones, transportation systems, websites, networks, and many other technologies every day. Most of these things may look simple when we use them, but behind them there are many different parts working together. Because modern systems are becoming more complicated, it is important to understand how all of their parts connect and affect one another. This is where systems thinking and systems engineering become useful.

Before learning about systems engineering, I mostly thought that solving a problem meant finding the part that was causing the problem and fixing it. After learning more about systems thinking, I realized that this approach does not always work. Sometimes changing one part of a system can create a new problem somewhere else. Systems thinking encourages us to look at the complete system instead of focusing only on one individual part.

Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) takes this idea even further by using models to represent and understand complicated systems. Instead of keeping important information spread across many separate documents, models can help engineers understand requirements, relationships, behaviors, and other important parts of a system. Holt and Weilkiens (2023) explain that modern systems engineering must deal with complexity, communication, and understanding. I think these ideas are especially important today because technology is becoming more connected and systems often depend on many different people, processes, and technologies.

Understanding Systems Thinking

Systems thinking is a way of looking at a problem as part of a larger system. Instead of examining every component separately, we look at how different components interact with each other. A system can contain people, technology, processes, information, hardware, software, and many other elements.

NASA explains that a system is made up of elements that work together to provide a capability or satisfy a need. The important point is that the value of the entire system comes not only from the individual parts but also from the relationships between those parts (Hirshorn et al., 2017). This helped me understand why systems thinking is sometimes described as looking at the "big picture."

A simple example is a university. A university is not just a building where students attend classes. It includes students, professors, administrators, classrooms, computers, online systems, registration systems, financial services, transportation, security, and many other parts. If the online registration system stops working, the problem can affect students, instructors, advisers, and administrators. Looking only at the computer that failed would not necessarily explain the complete problem.

The same idea applies to technology. A website, for example, may depend on servers, databases, networks, software, security systems, developers, and users. If users complain that a website is slow, there can be many possible causes. The problem could be the server, database, network connection, poorly designed software, or even a combination of these factors. Systems thinking encourages us to understand these relationships before deciding on a solution.

Why Systems Thinking Matters

One thing I found interesting about systems thinking is that a solution to one problem can sometimes create another problem. This happens because parts of a system are connected.

Imagine a company has a website that customers say is too slow. The company might immediately decide to buy faster servers. That sounds like a reasonable solution, but what if the actual problem is inefficient software or a database that is poorly designed? The company could spend a large amount of money and still have the same problem. A systems-thinking approach would first examine the relationships among the website, software, database, network, server, and users.

NASA describes systems engineering as a multidisciplinary approach covering the design, realization, technical management, operation, and eventual retirement of a system (Hirshorn et al., 2017). This is important because it shows that engineers need to think beyond simply building something. They must consider the entire life of the system.

I also think systems thinking is useful outside engineering. Businesses are systems, schools are systems, transportation networks are systems, and even communities can be viewed as systems. In a business, for example, changing employee schedules might affect productivity, customer service, employee satisfaction, and costs. A manager should consider these relationships before making a major decision.

For me, this is one of the biggest lessons from systems thinking. Problems should not always be separated from everything around them. Understanding the connections can help us make better decisions and avoid unexpected consequences.

Complexity and Communication

Another important idea from Systems Engineering Demystified is complexity. Modern systems can contain many different elements and relationships. Holt and Weilkiens (2023) discuss complexity, communication, and understanding as major concerns in systems engineering. I found communication especially important because even a technically good system can have problems if the people developing it do not understand one another.

Consider a large engineering project. Software developers may think about software requirements, electrical engineers may focus on electrical systems, managers may focus on schedules and budgets, and customers may focus on what the final product should do. All these people can be talking about the same system but looking at it differently.

This can create misunderstandings. A requirement that seems clear to one person may mean something different to another person. If the misunderstanding is discovered late in a project, fixing it could take additional time and money.

Systems engineering provides ways for teams to organize and communicate information more clearly. Instead of allowing each group to work completely independently, the goal is to understand how their work connects. This is another reason models can become valuable.

What Is Model-Based Systems Engineering?

Model-Based Systems Engineering, usually called MBSE, is an approach where models become an important part of systems engineering activities.

The Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge explains MBSE as the use of formalized system models to support systems engineering work throughout a system's life cycle. These models can support requirements, design, analysis, verification, and validation (Singam & Carter, 2026).

At first, I thought a model was simply a diagram or picture. After looking more closely at MBSE, I realized that it is much more than that. A system model can organize information about requirements, components, behaviors, relationships, and interfaces. It can help people see how the entire system fits together.

This is different from a traditional document-based approach. In a large project, information might be stored in many Word documents, spreadsheets, diagrams, emails, and presentations. If one requirement changes, several documents may need to be updated. If someone forgets to update one of them, different team members might end up working with conflicting information.

With MBSE, models can provide a more connected representation of the system. According to Singam and Carter (2026), MBSE can consolidate system information into system design models and help provide a more consistent source of technical information. To me, this seems especially useful for large projects where hundreds or thousands of requirements and components may be involved.

How Models Help Us Understand Complex Systems

Visualization is one of the parts of MBSE that I find most useful. Reading many pages of technical information can make it difficult to understand how everything is connected. A model can make those relationships easier to see.

Systems Engineering Demystified discusses modeling and visualization as important parts of MBSE. It also discusses the Systems Modeling Language, or SysML, which can be used to represent information about systems (Holt & Weilkiens, 2023).

For example, imagine designing an airplane. An airplane contains engines, electrical systems, navigation systems, communication systems, software, controls, safety equipment, and many other components. Each component also interacts with other components. A change to one area can potentially affect several others.

Using models can help engineers understand these connections before the actual system is completed. This can make it easier to identify missing requirements, conflicts, or other problems earlier in the development process.

NASA has also incorporated model-based approaches into systems engineering. Its Systems Engineering Handbook notes that MBSE is being used to improve the development and delivery of products (Hirshorn et al., 2017). This shows that MBSE is not simply a classroom idea. It has practical applications in organizations working on extremely complicated projects.

Benefits of MBSE

One major benefit of MBSE is improved communication. Different people can use a common model to understand how a system works instead of depending entirely on separate documents.

Another benefit is consistency. When information is spread across many documents, it can become difficult to determine which document contains the latest information. A model-based approach can make relationships between different pieces of information clearer.

MBSE can also support verification and validation. Verification asks whether the system was built according to its requirements, while validation considers whether the system actually satisfies its intended purpose. Models can help connect requirements to design decisions and later testing activities.

Another important benefit is managing change. Systems rarely remain exactly the same throughout development. Customers may change requirements, new technology may become available, or engineers may discover problems. When information is connected through a model, teams can have a better chance of understanding what other areas could be affected by a change.

The Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge explains that system models can act as a centralized source of system information and can help integrate knowledge across engineering disciplines (Singam & Carter, 2026). This is important because complicated systems usually require many specialists working together.

Challenges of MBSE

Even though MBSE has many advantages, I do not think it automatically solves every problem. A model is useful only when the information inside it is accurate and when people know how to use it.

One challenge is learning. Engineers and organizations that are used to traditional documents may need training before they can use MBSE effectively. Modeling languages and software tools can also take time to learn.

Another challenge is deciding how much detail should be included. A model with too little information might not be useful, while a model with too much information could become difficult to understand. Teams need to determine which information is important for their particular system.

There can also be organizational challenges. Moving from document-based engineering to MBSE is not simply a matter of purchasing new software. People may need to change how they communicate, manage information, and make decisions.

This made me realize that MBSE should be viewed as an approach rather than just a technology. Having a modeling program on a computer does not automatically mean that an organization is practicing good systems engineering. People still need clear requirements, good communication, accurate information, and an understanding of the system.

My Reflection

The most important thing I learned from this topic is that understanding relationships is just as important as understanding individual components.

Before studying systems thinking, I would usually approach a complicated problem by dividing it into smaller pieces. I still think breaking problems into smaller parts can be useful, but now I understand that we also have to reconnect those parts and examine their relationships.

I can see this being especially useful in information technology and cybersecurity. A cybersecurity problem, for example, is rarely about only one computer. Security depends on hardware, software, networks, user behavior, passwords, company policies, training, updates, and many other factors. An organization could purchase expensive security software and still experience a security incident if employees are not trained properly. Systems thinking would encourage us to examine the entire environment rather than assuming one technology can solve every problem.

MBSE adds another useful idea because it provides a structured way of representing complex information. Instead of expecting everyone to understand a large collection of independent documents, models can make relationships more visible.

The idea from Holt and Weilkiens (2023) that systems engineering must deal with complexity, communication, and understanding stood out to me. These three issues are connected. As a system becomes more complex, communicating information becomes more difficult. If communication becomes unclear, people's understanding of the system can also become different. Modeling can help reduce some of these problems by giving people a structured way to represent and discuss the system.

At the same time, I learned that the human side of systems engineering is important. Models and tools can support people, but people still have to communicate, make decisions, understand stakeholder needs, and determine whether a system actually solves the intended problem.

Conclusion

Systems thinking and Model-Based Systems Engineering provide useful ways to understand today's complicated systems. Systems thinking teaches us to look beyond individual parts and examine relationships, interactions, and the complete system. This can help us understand why problems occur and avoid solutions that fix one issue while creating another.

MBSE builds on this idea by using models to represent system information. Models can help teams organize requirements, communicate designs, understand relationships, manage changes, and support verification and validation. This becomes especially valuable as systems grow larger and involve more people and technologies.

After learning about these concepts, I see systems engineering differently. It is not simply about designing technical components. It is about understanding how people, processes, technology, requirements, and other elements work together to achieve a goal. I think this way of thinking will become even more valuable as technology continues to become more connected and complex.

The biggest lesson I am taking away is that solving complex problems requires more than understanding individual pieces. We also need to understand how those pieces influence one another. Systems thinking provides the big-picture perspective, while MBSE provides tools and models that can make that perspective easier to communicate and manage.
