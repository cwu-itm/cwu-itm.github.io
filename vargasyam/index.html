What Is System Thinking

After reading Systems Engineering Demystified, I got a better understanding of how system thinking works. It means understanding a system as a whole, while also paying attention to the relationships between its different parts. Holt explains that systems elements should not be considerate in isolation because they interact with other elements, and understanding these connections is an important part of system engineering (Holt, 2019). In my own words, systems thinking means trying to understand how everything fits together instead of focusing on only one piece of a problem. A system can contain different sections, and those sections can affect each other. For example, an online restaurant system, is not simply a website. It includes the restaurant information, menu, contact information, database, customer information, networks, security controls, payment system, and other outside services. All of these elements interact in one way or another, and if one-part changes another system may be affected. For example, changing the payment method could improve security, but it could affect how customer pay and it may cause some confusion. Another important part of system thinking is understanding the systems environment and stakeholders. Different people can look at the same system and have different needs or expectations, and system thinking can help manage different perspectives and bring them together in the best possible way (Morganelli, 2024). For example, if a customer is using a banking application may care about user friendly navigation, while a cybersecurity professional may be more concerned with authentication, access control, and protecting customer information. On the other hand, a system administrator may be concern about keeping the service available. While these perspectives are not necessarily wrong, they are just simply different views of the same system. In the reading Holt explains that stakeholders can have different perspectives and that these need to be considerate when developing a system (Holt, 2019).

Systems Thinking vs Linear Thinking

Systems thinking is different form a traditional way of thinking, because linear thinking often breaks a problem into small pieces and looks at these pieces separately. This approach can be useful when dealing with simple problems. For example, if my computer would not turn on, I can troubleshoot it in a logical order. I will first check the power cable, then the outlet, power supply, and finally the computer hardware. This step by step can help me identify the problem faster. However, complex systems do not always behave in such a simple way. There could be many connections and dependencies between components which means that fixing one problem can sometimes create a different problem in another area. Systems thinking looks at the bigger picture. Instead of only asking what cause the problems? It also asks what other parts of the systems is connected to this issue? And what would happen if we changed this part? System thinking focuses on how components, interfaces, and other parts interact with the overall systems, while also considering how the systems interact with other systems around it. Dr, Morganelli (2024) stated that system thinking not only helps understand complex problems but, also improves decision -making procedures, and promotes greatest solutions by considering the interconnections and interdependencies of the system. This is important to understand because complex systems can have connections that are not obvious, and a small change can sometimes produce a larger effect somewhere else.

Why Systems Thinking is Important for Digital Systems/h3>

According to Stafford (2025) systems thinking has become increasingly important because digital systems are becoming more connected and complicated. A modern system is often not a single application or computer. It depends on multiple systems such as cloud services, databases, networks, APIs, user devices, cybersecurity controls, and other third-party services. Each of these different components usually belongs to different organizations which makes it more difficult to understand the systems by examining each part separately. In the reading Holt (2019) provides a useful example by comparing an older automobile with a modern automobile. Older cars were mostly made up of mechanical and electrical components, while modern cars contain many more electronics components and large amounts of software systems. Modern vehicles also communicate with other system such as cloud services, satellites, and smart roads. As a result, the complexity of the overall system has increased. In the digital word we can compare this example with a smartphone. Before, it used to be just a land line phone capable of just making and receiving calls. Now a smartphone contains multiple dependencies and systems such as the main operating system, applications, wireless network, cloud services, security systems, and many other systems that by working together have created this powerful portable minicomputer capable of doing incredible things, beyond receiving and making phone calls. Complexity, communication, and understanding are the three major causes of systems failure. When people do not have the same understanding of a system, misunderstanding can occur and lead to additional problems and more confusion. In digital systems different teams may use different terminology or focus on different parts of a project (Patel, 2023). A network administrator, software developer, cybersecurity analyst, and other professionals may understand the same project differently therefore, having a way to clearly present the system can help these groups to communicate better and have the same understanding of the project.

Why a Model-Based Approach Is Useful

In a traditional approach, a lot of the information about a system is stored in different documents. This can create problems because information can become outdated or inconsistent. In Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE), the model becomes the main representation of the system. In the reading Holt (2019) explains that the model is an abstraction or a simplified representation of the actual system. The main goal of this approach is not to include every possible piece of information but to include enough useful information to help successfully develop the system. One of the biggest advantages of this approach is that it gives people a common way to understand a complicated system. A digital model or simulation is capable of showing the systems components, relationships, requirements, behavior, and interfaces. Different stakeholder can the observe at the different views of the same model depending on what information they need (IBM, 2023). Holt explains that views are collections of information intended for specific stakeholders and that the views should remain consistent with one another (Holt, 2019). Other research also supports the usefulness of this approach. NASA has used MBSE to help manage complex systems and found benefits such as improved communication, knowledge transfer, and the ability to examine a system as a whole instead of only looking at the individual pieces (Hoffpauir, 2024). NASA also describes MBSE as using digital model to better describe systems and evaluate system performance, which shows why a model-based approach fits well with modern digital engineer. Another benefit of models is that it can help identify problems earlier. If a model shows that two components depend on each other in a way that creates a problem, engineers are more likely to recognize the issue before the system is fully built (Hoffpauir, 2024). This can save time and resources because finding a problem during the design stage can be easer that finding it after implementation (IBM, 2023). Other research have also identified that model-based approaches are a great way to improve consistency and manage design change across the life cycle of complex systems.

Conclusion

Overall, systems thinking is a way of looking beyond individual components and trying to understand the entire system, including its parts, relationships, environment, stakeholders, and behavior. It differs form linear thinking because it does not assume that every problem can be understood trough a simple step-by-step procedure. This is especially important for modern digital systems because they contain many connected components and often interact with other systems. Model based systems engineering provides a useful way to apply system thinking by creating a simplified model that brings important system information and connections together. The model is able to help teams communicate better, identify complexity, independent dependencies, and make better informed design decisions. After analyzing and reading these concepts I can now understand that solving technology problems requires looking beyond the problem itself and understand the large system around it. This is a useful way of thinking that I can apply in my future IT or cybersecurity work.

Systems Development Life Cycle

Digital systems are part of almost every area of modern life. Business use computers systems to manage information, schools used it for online platforms to teach and support students, and organizations depend on networks and security tools to protect their data. Even though these systems look different and have different purpose, they all need planning, design, development, testing, implementation, and maintenance. These activities are connected trough a process commonly known as the System Development Life Cycle (SDLC). The SDLC provides a structures way to plan and manage a system from its initial idea through its development, use, and termination. Understanding the life cycle of these systems is important because building a system involves more than making technology work. Engineers and developers must understand the need of users, identify limitations, manage risk, and consider how changes to one part of a systems may affect other sections. In the assigned reading Holt (2023) explains the different life cycle models and how they organize the stages involved in developing and using the systems. The linear, iterative, and incremental models each provide a different way to organize it. Just like Holt (2023) mentioned, choosing the right model depends on the project size, complexity, requirements and expected changes.

What is a System Development Life Cycle

The system development life cycle is a structured approach for organizing the work required to create, deliver, and maintain a system. Depending on the project, this work may include identifying a problem, gathering requirements, designing a solution, building the system, test it, putting it to use, and maintaining and doing improvements over time. The purpose of this life cycle is to make sure that the final system meets the required user needs (GeeksforGeeks). In the reading Holt discusses that these stages can be arranged in different ways depending on the type of system being develop. For example, a project with clear requirements may follow a more sequential approach, while a complex project may benefit from repeating stages and doing improvements on release stages. A life cycle is not just a checklist. Each stage produces information that can affect how the other stages behave. Systems requirements influence the design, the design affects implementation, and testing may reveal that an early decision needs to be reconsider. And even after implementation, feedback can create new needs. Systems engineering is the process that connects design, realization, integration, verification, and validation. These stages ensure that the system is built correctly and that it meets the stakeholder expectations. This way of thinking is extremely important when developing digital systems because technology does not remain unchanged. Every type of software requires updates, cybersecurity threats develop constantly, users expect new features, and hardware eventually becomes outdated. A life cycle approach helps teams plan for future needs instead of leaving the end project as is.

The Linear Life Cycle Model

The linear life cycle model organizes project stages in sequence. Each stage is generally completed before the next stage begins, and returning to a previous stage is not usually part of the main process (Holota, 2025). Holt (2023) explains that this model is most appropriate when requirements are clearly defined, the technology is understood, and the project is relatively small or predictable. The waterfall model is a well-known example of a linear approach. According to Holota (2025) one major advantage of the linear model is that it easy to understand and manage. Project teams can establish clear goals, schedules, responsibilities, and checkpoint for each stage. Because this type of approach/project is planned in advance, managers may find it easier to estimate the resources and time required. This model works well when the organization is familiar with the system and understands what the systems will do and do not expect major updates. For example, is a start-up business needs a simple internal tool to calculate employee work hours, they should already have the requirements clear form the beginning. Engineers can document the needs, design the tool, develop it, test it, and implemented according to a set schedule. However, the linear model also has disadvantages. Because the stages follow a set order, discovering a major problem late in development can require the team to revisit and maybe change earlier decisions. Which could lead to delays, extra cost, and additional labor. Another limitation that this model has is that the complete product is generally deliver at the end, meaning that users will not receive the system until the project is finish. In the reading Holt mentions that this makes the model less suitable for large complex project where needs are likely to change or where waiting for final release creates difficulties Sandeepani, 2020).

The Iterative Life Cycle Model

The iterative lifecycle model develops a system through repeated cycle, called interactions. Instead of completing the stages only once, the team revisits them to improve its understanding and refine the solution. In type of model each iteration can produce a workable version of the system, with later versions becoming more complete or improved. The iterative approach is commonly used when developing internal tools, where teams try the tool, offer feedback and engineers evolve each version (Holota, 2025). A key advantage of this iterative model is that it allows teams to learn while developing the system. Users can test an early version, identify problems, and explain what should be changed, which developers can then use this information during a later iteration. This model can be described as repeating development activities and revising earlier work when needed and is valuable when a project involves uncertainty or when user feedback is required and may change the problem perspective (Sandeepani, 2020). A great example of this model is when a school is developing an online student portal. At firs the system could be a simple working version which would allow the student to do the basic stuff such as view their grades, schedules, and homework. Then after the students try it, they may have constructive feedback about the lay out and interface, this could be that the schedule is in hidden links, and that they want an easier access to course information. In this example instead of waiting until the entire portal is complete to discover these problems, engineers can learn from this early feedback and make adjustments along the way. The iterative model also has its disadvantages, repeated cycle required time, coordination, testing, and communication. If users frequently change their expectations, the development team may struggle to decide which improvement matter most. Holt (2021) warns that changing stakeholder need can create problems when the process for defining and managing those needs is not strong. Another disadvantage of this model is that the risk of focusing too much of delivery the next version on time, instead od making sure it works properly is bigger. Another important point for engineers to have in mind when using this type of approach is that iteration does not automatically guarantee quality. A development team can repeat the development process multiple times and still make poor decisions if the feedback is not evaluated carefully or if the system is not tested properly. In my opinion, iteration is most useful when each stage has a clear purpose. Teams should know what they are trying to improve, how they will test the change, and how they will evaluate if the new version is better. Repetition without leaning can waste resources rather than improve the system/project.

The Incremental Life Cycle Model

The incremental life cycle model develops and delivers a system in separate portions or increments (Holota, 2025). Instead of delivery the full system in one final release, engineers provide useful solutions over time. Holt (2023) explains that an incremental model can deliver a partial system early, with later releases adding other part of the overall solution. This type of approach is better for long projects where users/organizations need some functionality before all development work is finish. One advantage of the incremental model is that users can begin using the system earlier. The organization does not have to wait until every feature, and detail is ready before using any part of the system. Early releases can also help teams identify integration problems and lean which features are more useful. Incremental development adds user visibility functionality trough successive usable version of a product. A perfect example of this model is if a hospital is looking to create a new customer management system and ask the engineers to first release a simple system that would allow employees to view basic patient records. Then after some time they could release the appointment scheduling section, and later on add supporting resources. This type of model would allow hospital employees to being using the first part of the system while the team continues developing the remaining features. This can make a long project easier to manage because progress becomes visible through working releases. However, in incremental development is not appropriate for all kinds of system. Not every system can be easy divided into separate parts. If the components of a system depend heavily on one another, delivering only part of the system will not provide useful functionality or may create integration challenges. Engineers must carefully plan how each increment will work with the rest of the system. They need to consider security, testing, data consistency, and compatibility as new parts are added. Other risk that this type of approach has, is that if not well explain the first version of the system may be mistaken for a complete solution. If an organization releases a basic system without clearly communicating its limitations, users may expect features that are not yet available.

Why Life Cycle Thinking Matters for Digital Systems

Life cycle is important because a digital system is more than the software or hardware that users see. It includes people, information, processes, inertances, security requirements, and the environment in which it operates. A change to one part can affect other parts. As simple as just adding a new log in feature can not only improve the security of the system, but it could also change way users access it, and how IT technicians troubleshot related problems. During the design stage engineers need to understand the need of users, how different parts of a system will work together and what problems may occur in the future. Planning carefully from the beginning can reduce errors while preventing expensive changes later. Life cycle thinking is also important for sustainability because digital systems required ongoing maintenance, updates, and resources after they are implemented. A system that is difficult to maintain may become expensive to operate or may need to be replaced sooner than expected. By considering future needs during the design stage, developers can create system that are easier to repair, update, and improve, with the need of replacing the whole thing. Finally, life cycle thinking support the evolution of digital systems because technology, user expectations, and security threats continue to change. A system that meets users’ needs today may not be as useful several years from now. Systems engineers must be prepared to adapt and create new technologies.

Conclusion

After reading and analyzing these chapters I learned that each model has advantages and disadvantages therefore, the best choice depends on the project needs. This module also shows that developing a system is not just about making it work at the beginning, it is also important to keep it secure, fix problems and make updates as technology and user needs change. By thinking about the systems entire life cycle, teams can create systems that work well and remain useful for a long time.

What Does it Mean to Desing for People?/h3>

When people think about design, they often focus on how something looks. A website might have attractive colors, a modern layout, and interesting images, but that does not automatically make it a good design. If users cannot find the information they need, understand the instructions, or complete a task without frustrations, the design has failed its main goal. Good design is not just about appearance, it’s about making products, services, and technology understandable, useful, and accessible to the people who depend on or use them. According to Landry (2020) Human-centered design (HCD) is an approach to problem-solving that designs with understanding the people who will use a product or service. Instead of making assumptions about wat users want, designers study their need, behaviors, abilities, and challenges before developing a solution. The International Organization for Standardization emphasizes that human-centered design should guide the development of interactive systems throughout their life cycle. Similarly, Friss Dam and Siang Teo (2026) encourage designers to build empathy with the people their server, explore differ ideas & point of view, create prototypes, and improve solutions through feedback. Two important concepts that support this approach are perception and attention. Perception influences how people interpret what they see while attention determines which information they notice and process. Empathy helps designers understand why these experiences may differ from one person to another. Together, these concepts explain why a successful user experience (UX) requires more than attractive visual or advance technology. It requires understanding how people actually think, feel, and behave. In designing with the Mind in Mind, Jonson (2020) explains how knowledge of human perception and mental processes can guide interface design. Yablonski (2024), in laws of UX, connects principles form phycology to practical design decisions. These perspectives demonstrate that designers can create better experiences when they work with the human abilities and limitations instead of expecting people to adapt to confusing systems.

Comparison of ISO 9241-210 and IDEOs Field Guide

Although ISO 9241-210 and IDESO Field Guide to Human-Centered Deign share the goal of designing around people’s needs, they approach the subject from different perspectives. ISO provides a formal framework for planning and managing human-centered design, while IDEO offers a practical, creative approach to discover problems and developing solutions. ISO 9241-210 is an international standard that provides requirements and recommendations for human-centered design through the lifecycle of interactive systems. These systems can include websites, mobile applications, baking systems, and other technologies that people interact with. One of the central ideas of this perspective is that designers should understand users, their tasks, and the environments in which they will use a system (Franz & Kauer-Franx, 2024). For example, an application designed for an office employee working on monitor may not work equally well for some using their phone. The phone user might experience glare, have limited access to all the features, and be unable to do complete tasks. A design that ignores these conditions may function technically but still be difficult to use. The ISO perspective also emphasizes involving users through the design process, evaluating designs based on user needs, and improving the system through interaction. In other words, design should not end when developers finish building a product. The product should be evaluated to determine whether it helps people accomplish their goals over time. I believe this approach is especially important because organizations sometimes measure success manly through technical performance such as cost, or the number of features delivered. These measures are important, but they do not necessarily demonstrate usability. A website may load quickly and contain many useful features, yet users may struggle to complete a basic task. Human centered evaluation helps organizations identify this difference between a system that works and a system that works well for people.

IDEOs Field Guide Perspective

IDEOs (2015) Human Centered Desing takes a more hands-on and creative approach. This perspective organizes the design process into three main phases, inspiration, ideation, and implementation. The inspiration phase focuses on understanding people trough observation, interviews, and direct engagement. Instead of immediately deciding what to build, designers investigate how people experience a problem in their everyday lives. The ideation phase involves identifying opportunities, generating ideas, and developing prototypes, during implementation, the team test, improves, and brings promising solutions into practice (IDEO, 2015). For example, if a university wants to improve its online class registration system. The university might assume that students need a more attractive interface. However, interviews and observations could reveal that students and faculty are actually confused by unfamiliar course codes, unclear graduation requirements, or registration deadlines are difficult to locate, the real problem is not necessary the appearance of the website, is the difficulty of completing the registration smoothly. IDEO’s approach encourages designers to discover these problems before choosing a solution, it also reminds them that the individuals experiencing the problem have valuable knowledge that designers may noy possess.

Comparing the Two Perspectives

The main difference is that ISO 9241-201 establishes a structured framework for human-centered design, while IDEO’s Field Guide emphasizes practical methods for understanding people and exploring possible solutions. ISO helps organizations establish a disciplined design process, where IDEO provides techniques for learning from users and turning those insights into ideas. However, the two approaches are complementary rather than competing. Designers could use IDEO’s interviews and prototypes to discover what users need, then apply ISO principles to evaluate the problematic system through development and after launch. Designers need to understand that human-centered design requires both structure and creativity. Empathy helps designers discover the right problem, while evaluation helps determine whether the proposed solution actually solves it.

How Perception Shapes Usability

Perception is the process trough which people interpret information received trough their senses. In interface design, visual perception influences how users recognized text, distinguish icons, identify connection between elements, and understand what they should do next. Johnson (2020) explains why understanding human perception is essential when designing interfaces that people can understand and use affectively. Is also important to know that people do not process every visual element equally. The size, color, position, spacing, and grouping of information can influence how an interface is interpreted. Designers must consider these factors because an interface can communicate a different message form the one its creator intended. A great example of these would be if a banking app had the “Pay Bill” and “Cancel Payment” button right next to each other and have similar colors, users might have difficulty distinguishing the actions, particularity when they are in a hurry. If someone accidently selects the wrong button, the consequences could be frustrating or financially significant. A better design would make the intended action clear, provide sufficient spacing, and clearly distinguish the actions without making the interface confusing. Visual hierarchy is the arrangement of interface elements to communicate their relative importance. Designers create hierarchy trough differences in size, contrast, color, spacing, and placement (Johnson, 2020). These choices help users recognize what deserves attention first. For example, imagine an online shopping website where a customer wants to buy a pair of shoes. If the price, product name, and add to cart button are difficult to find among advertisements and decorative images, the customer would become frustrated and leave the site. Making the important buttons easy to see, it allows customers to focus on the information they need and complete their task without trouble. As Jonhson (2020) explain understanding how people perceive visual information helps designers create interfaces that are easier to use

The Four Laws of UX

Attention is limited. People often use digital products while multitasking, feeling stressed, or trying to finish a task quickly. A design that demands unnecessary steps increase the effort required to accomplish even the simplest tasks. Four particularly useful principles to combat this are Jakob’s Law, Fitts Law, Hick’s Law, and Miller’s Law. Each law provides a different way to understand user behavior and make smarter design decisions (Yablonski, 2024). Jakob’s Law explains that people often prefer websites and applications that work similarly to those they have used before (Yablonski, 2024). When users are familiar with a design, they do not have to spend as much time learning how it works. For example, people expect a shopping website to have shopping cart icon where they can review the items they want to buy. If a website uses familiar features and places them where user expects to find them, people can navigate easier. Fitt’s Law explain that larger buttons and controls that are easier to reach are generally quicker and easer to use. Designers should make important buttons large enough to select comfortably and place them where users can find and reach them easily. This is especially important for people with limited motor abilities or those using devices with small screens. Hick’s Law explains that the more choices people have, the longer it can take them to decide what to choose. For example, is a restaurant website display’s every food item on one crowded page, customers may struggle to decide what they want. Organizing the menu into categories such as main dishes, dessert, drinks, etc., would make decision making easier. Miller’s Law is commonly used in UX design to explain why organizing information into smaller, meaningful groups can make it easier for people to process and remember (Yablonski, 2024). For example, a clinic form asking for a person name, address, contact information, and payment details may feel overwhelming if everything appears on one screen. Dividing them into sections with clear headings makes it easier to understand and complete. The idea that people can hold about seven items in working memory is just a foundation, this number should not be treated as a strict rule for designing interfaces.

Conclusion

Designing for people means recognizing that technology is only useful when people can understand it, access it, and use it to accomplish their goals. It is also an ongoing responsibility rather than one-time activity. User needs change, technologies evolve, and new problems emerge. Designers must be willing to listen, question their assumptions, test their ides, and make improvements. The best design is not the one that looks impressive, it’s the one that helps people accomplish what matters to hem with clarity and as few unnecessary berries as possible.
