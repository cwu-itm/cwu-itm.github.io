Part 1: Overview of Systems Thinking, Systems Engineering, and MBSE

This week, we began our first module of the quarter about abstracting systems. We picked up learning about systems thinking, systems engineering, and a type of systems engineering called MBSE – model-based systems engineering.

Before diving into systems engineering, I want to first explore the idea of systems thinking. Systems thinking can be roughly defined as a method of thinking that considers all parts of a system within context of the system as a whole (Lutkevich, 2025). This is different compared to a more traditional way of thinking that breaks a system down into individual parts and analyzes them separately. Systems thinking can be applied to just about anything, making it an incredibly useful and versatile mindset to adopt.

When it comes to engineering, this term itself is rather broad. The way I’ve chosen to think about it relative to this course, is the development of digital environments. Rather than thinking about a physical system, which tends to come to mind first, we’re thinking about virtual ones and how we can design them in a way that accomplishes all of our goals. Systems engineering takes this idea and thoroughly builds upon it. It creates a framework that can be used to engineer new digital environments or system components that are well designed, user-friendly, extremely functional, and nicely secure. As technology evolves, it’s becoming more important to put this extra level of care into digital spaces. People spend more time online now than ever, so we need to design our virtual spaces and applications with new goals in mind. Systems engineering helps us do this.

With that said, let’s take a deeper look into systems engineering. The first thing the textbook does is define a system. There are many different kinds of systems, these being:

  • Natural systems
  • Designed physical systems
  • Designed abstract systems
  • Human activity systems
  • Transcendental systems (Holt, 2021)

Sorting systems into these categories makes it easier to evaluate the characteristics of each system. A naturally occurring system for example, may have components that a human designed system may not.

The next thing to look at would be what characteristics a system can have. A system is made of elements, and interacting elements or attributes are what make up the system as a whole. Other factors we are encouraged to examine are the stakeholders for the system.

Who is invested and why? Whether a person or organization, stakeholders play a role in how the system can be built and operated. Then there are also boundaries that define the scope of the system. These can be physical, conceptual, or defined by stakeholders. Finally, we have constraints that in some way, limit the system. All of these concepts combined play a role in how a system functions and how we think about the system as part of the systems thinking process.

Systems engineering takes all of this and applies it to the development of systems in a way that encompasses much more than a traditional way of thinking could. It’s not linear, with all of the above mentioned concepts playing an equally important role and facilitating better decision making overall.

Another great benefit to systems engineering is the fact that it can be used at any and all stages of a system’s life cycle. Many systems of today’s world are constantly changing and evolving to suit changing technology and social norms. Systems engineering will take this into account and consider future possibilities for change rather than viewing a system as stagnant and unchanging once it can operate.

It’s pretty clear that systems engineering has some perks. As a whole, it’s a well developed idea and is utilized in many professional areas. This said, there are what the textbook describes as ‘evils’ that can hinder systems engineering. These are understanding, communication, and complexity (Holt, 2021). To deepen our understanding of systems engineering this week, we also learned about a specific type of systems engineering called MBSE or model-based systems engineering. This has the ability to mitigate these evils using a model. As the name implies, models are used to help visualize a system and all of the attributes the system needs in order to succeed.

This idea of model-based systems engineering is largely replacing the more traditional document-centric approach (Simon, n.d.). As it has evolved, systems engineering started with more text-based documentation and has shifted to visual models with time. Models do a better job of mapping out the intricacies of a system and showing how attributes work together. Since this is an important part of the idea of systems thinking, it makes sense that the model-based approach has seen more success.

Part 2: Personal Thoughts and Reflections

The readings from this week built upon our initial introduction to systems thinking and engineering from a previous class. What was newest to me was the idea of model-based systems engineering. From the perspective of a learning college student, I find that visualizations are much more helpful than pure text. Combined, they are powerful tools.

As a cybersecurity major, I was mentally working to tie what we learned to my future career. One of the things I thought about was how designing systems with security at the forefront would be easier when using a model-based approach. It becomes easier to identify the constraints and boundaries that you will be working with. Whether it’s designing software, a new website, or other online platform, security is becoming more relevant as new exploits ae found every day. More information is stored online as well, making hacks and cyber threats more of an issue. Creating a safe environment is equally, if not more important than creating a user-friendly or otherwise well-designed one in my opinion.

Some of the other reflections I had involved artificial intelligence, or AI. With the rising use and prominence of generative AI tools, we could likely use AI to assist with model-based systems engineering. AI is getting much better at creating accurate visualizations. Using AI to assist with the process of creating a model, identifying the system attributes, etc could be a good application. I also thought about how AI might create a shift in the current evolution trajectory of MBSE. From a machine learning perspective, would it make more sense to emphasize a visual model over text? I believe that for humans, this is more often than not going to be the case. For AI however, it might be slightly different.

Another consideration I had was thinking about how I, as a non-professional, college student, could actually make use of these learnings in my normal life. Reading through the materials, I felt that it was honestly quite complex. There were a ton of terms that I hadn’t heard before and to an extent, it honestly felt overly complicated. I’m sure that for a complex system, a very complex and in-depth model and framework would be appropriate. But for me, at this point in my life at least, I feel my own applications of MBSE to any work I do will be somewhat limited. I could see the appeal in using some of the basic concepts as a way to streamline my design process but it would be rather rudimentary compared to the full process described in our textbook.

Systems thinking on the other hand, seems very applicable to every day life and not only the process of designing a system. So many things in our lives are systems, whether we’re talking about our own bodies or the school we attend pretty much everything has multiple parts to it and thinking about the bigger picture feels like a good practice no matter what.

Part 3: Summary and Final Thoughts

To summarize, this week we learned about systems thinking, systems engineering, and MBSE. Systems thinking involves considering the whole system and how parts work together as opposed to thinking of components or attributes individually. Systems engineering takes this idea and uses it to help realize successful systems. While there are many types of systems, this principle works well for all of them. Model-based systems engineering is a type of systems engineering that emphasizes the use of visual models to illustrate the parts, constraints, boundaries, and elements of the system in order to help us better understand them. When compared to more traditional versions of systems engineering that rely heavily on text or written documents, MBSE has a lot of value and is largely replacing system engineering methods that were more document-centric.

From the perspective of an IT student, I was able to tie much of these ideas to my own future career. Even if it might be more in the long-term for the more complex ideas surrounding MBSE. I also thought of implications that they might have pertaining specifically to cybersecurity. Creating a model to help you keep an overall goal in mind that relates to digital security would help you keep priorities straight. I also thought about how systems engineering might change or evolve given the rise of artificial intelligence systems. Systems thinking is easily applicable to every-day life and has valuable lessons for anyone willing to reevaluate how they think about systems. A component of a system, no matter how small, is one part of a whole – and viewing it this way rather than as a separate entity helps the system feel more cohesive.
