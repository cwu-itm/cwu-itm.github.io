Understanding Systems Thinking

When we think about engineering, we often imagine designing a mechine, developing software or building a product. Successful engineering is not only about making individual components work. It is about ensuring that all components,people,processes and technologies work together as one complete system.

A system can appear successful at the individual component level but still fail as a whole.This is why systems thinking and MBSE are important. In my understanding, Syatems thinking teaches us to look beyond individual parts and understand their relationships while MBSE provides a structured way to represent, communicate and manage those relationships throughout a system's lifecycle

What is Model Based Systems Engineering ?

A system is a collection of connected elements that work together to achieve a particular purpose. These elements may include people hardware, software, processes and information. Understanding a system requires examining its elements, stakeholders, attributes boundaries, needs and constraints (Holt, 2019).

For example, consider a hospital's information technology system. It is not simply a computer network or electronic health record software. It includes patient databases, medical devices, healthcare professionals, cybersecurity controls, network infrastructure and administrative processes.

If one part fails, the impact may spread throughout the organization. A network outage could prevent healthcare professionals from accessing patient records, delay communication and affect daily operations. This example demonstrates that systems thinking is about recognizing relationships and consequences rather than focusing only on isolated problems.

My key insight is that solving one problem does not always mean improving the entire system. Sometimes, improving one component creates new problems elsewhere. Therefore, engineers must consider the broader environment, stakeholder needs, and possible unintended consequences before making decisions.

Managing Complexity, Communication and Context

There are three major challenges of systems engineering: complexity, communication and context. These challenges explain why engineering a large system is more difficult than simply connecting its individual parts (Holt, 2019).

Complexity: Modern systems contain many interconnected components. A small change in one component can affect other parts of the system.

Communication: Different stakeholders may have different technical knowledge and priorities. Without clear communication, a team may develop something that does not fully meet the user's needs.

Context: A system does not operate in isolation. It interacts with users, organizations, regulations and other systems. Engineers must understand these external relationships before making decisions.

From Documents to Connected Models

Traditional systems engineering often relies on separate documents, spreadsheets and diagrams. When a requirement changes, several documents may need to be updated manually. This can create confusion, outdated information and communication gaps.

Model Based Systems Engineering addresses this challenge by using a connected system model as a central source of information. MBSE uses models to represent system structure, behavior, requirements and relationships. A model is not the actual system. Instead, it is a simplified representation that helps people understand and analyze the real system (Holt, 2019).

For example, when designing a hospital cybersecurity system, engineers could use Systems Modeling Language diagrams to represent the network structure, connected devices, security requirements, access controls and responses to security incidents.

Understanding Models, Views and Ontology

Another concept I found interesting is the relationship between models, views and ontology. A model represents the overall system, while a view presents specific information for a particular stakeholder.

All these views should represent the same underlying system without contradicting each other. Ontology is important because it establishes shared concepts and relationships. A common vocabulary helps ensure that everyone understands what the model represents.

Why MBSE Matters in Modern Engineering

As systems become more interconnected, managing requirements and relationships through separate documents becomes increasingly challenging. The International Council on Systems Engineering describes MBSE as the formal application of modeling to support requirements, design, analysis, verification and validation throughout a system's lifecycle.

How MBSE Improves Communication and Decision-Making

One of the most useful benefits of Model-Based Systems Engineering is that it creates a shared understanding among different stakeholders. In a large project, software developers, network engineers, managers, security analysts and end users may all have different expectations. When information is shared only through long documents or emails, misunderstandings can occur. MBSE uses models and diagrams to represent system components, relationships, requirements and behavior in a visual format. For example, a hospital cybersecurity team could use a system model to show how patient records move between a medical workstation, an application server and a database. This model could help technical staff understand where security controls should be applied and help managers understand why certain investments are necessary. However, a model is only useful when it is accurate, updated and understandable to the people using it.

Systems Thinking in Healthcare Cybersecurity

Systems thinking is especially important in healthcare because hospitals depend on many connected systems to provide safe and effective patient care. For example, an electronic health record system is not just a software application. It connects doctors, nurses, patients, medical devices, databases, networks and administrative departments. If one part of this system fails, it can affect the entire hospital. A network outage may prevent medical staff from accessing patient records, while a cybersecurity attack could expose sensitive patient information or interrupt medical services. Systems thinking helps security professionals understand these relationships instead of focusing only on individual computers or applications. By examining the entire environment, organizations can identify risks, understand how one failure may affect other components and develop better security plans. This approach also reminds us that technology, people and procedures must work together to protect healthcare information.

Is MBSE Always the Solution?

Although MBSE provides important advantages, I do not believe that simply adopting modeling software guarantees a successful project. A model is only as reliable as the information, assumptions and people behind it.

If a model is outdated, incomplete or too complicated, it may create additional confusion instead of solving communication problems. Teams also need training, agreed modeling practices and suitable tools.

Therefore, I believe engineers should focus on creating models that answer real questions rather than creating diagrams just to satisfy a process. The purpose of MBSE should be better understanding and decision-making not simply replacing documents with software.

Challenges and Limitations of Model-Based Systems Engineering

Although MBSE offers many advantages, it is not a perfect solution for every project. Creating detailed models requires time, technical knowledge, appropriate tools and cooperation among stakeholders. Smaller organizations may not have enough resources or trained employees to implement a complete MBSE process. Another challenge is that models can become outdated when system requirements or technologies change. If team members continue using an old model, it may create confusion rather than improve communication. In cybersecurity, this could be especially dangerous because networks, applications and threats change frequently. I believe organizations should begin with the most important parts of a system and gradually expand their models as needed. The goal should not be to create the largest or most complicated model. Instead, the goal should be to create useful models that support understanding, decision-making and system improvement.

My Perspective on Systems Thinking and MBSE

As an IT Management student focusing on cybersecurity, I believe systems thinking and MBSE can help me prepare for future responsibilities in information security. Cybersecurity is not limited to installing antivirus software, configuring firewalls or monitoring suspicious network activity. Security professionals must understand how business operations, users, networks, applications and security policies depend on one another. For example, when designing security for a hospital, protecting patient data is important but maintaining access to critical medical systems is equally important. A security control that is too restrictive could interrupt the work of healthcare professionals. This demonstrates why security decisions must consider the entire system and its stakeholders. In my opinion, the biggest lesson from this module is that effective engineering requires more than technical knowledge. It requires understanding relationships, communicating clearly and considering the consequences of decisions before implementing them.
