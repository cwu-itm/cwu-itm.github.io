Systems Thinking and Model-Based Systems Engineering

What is System Thinking ?

When thinking about system thinking, it is best defined as an approach that focuses on understanding how various components of a system interact and the relationship between them. To best understand how system thinking works, its best to look at the characteristics and elements of a system. The book, Systems Engineering Demystified states that a system is made up of a set of system elements, which are comprised of two types. Those two types are the system interest and the enabling system. System of interest refers to a system that is under development, and enabling system refers to any system that has an interest in or interacts with a system of interest. It is important to note that a system element can be much more complex and can be broken down into lower-level system elements and develop a hierarchy for a specific system.

System Elements

Looking at how these system elements interact, the connection between system elements also allows for interfaces to be both identified and defined. Moving onto Attributes of a system, they essentially describe the system and the system elements. They are typically represented as nouns that can also take on numbers of different values, types, or units. They can also be more complex like timestamps or data structure. The boundaries simply serve to identify the scope of the system. The boundaries may be physical, conceptual, or stakeholder boundaries. Physical boundaries can come in the form of an enclosure on the system that separates it from the outside world. Conceptual boundaries are non-physical boundaries that cannot be observed, and its important to identify where that boundary may be. Stakeholder boundaries is the difference in view from different stakeholders, and that can mean that each stakeholder may see the boundaries in different areas. The key of overcoming a stakeholder boundary is to find common ground between stakeholders and work withing their point of views to match them and deliver a system every stakeholder can accept. The needs are the purpose of a system or of a system element. The needs can be in the form of requirements, features, and goals. A requirement need can come in the form of a statement of something that is desired for a system to do and are most commonly tied to the functionality of a system. A feature need related to a collection of functions that may be needed and dont have to be tied to a specific function. A goal need represents a need for the overall system and what it is designed and built to accomplish. Moving onto the constraints serve as limiting the realization of a system and serve to limit how a system is realized. They can be quality, implementation, environmental, and safety. Quality restraints are applicable to almost every system. These quality constraints relate to the use of best practices like standards and serve to identify all the standards and shape how the system is developed. Implementation constraints limit how a system can be built in limiting the materials used. Environmental constraints apply to where a system is deployed and is often dictated by the environment in which a system is deployed and can lead to certain constraints. Finally, safety constraints apply to almost all systems as they ensure a system can function in a safe way and prevent any errors tat may be detrimental to the success of a system. Overall, system thinking requires a holistic approach to best utilize all parts of a system and its elements..

System Thinking Vs. Linear Thinking

System thinking differs from a more linear traditional ways of thinking in that linear ways of thinking revolve around a step-by-step approach to problem solving, and a focus on individual parts. system thinking takes the system and its complexity all into account. The article, Systems Thinking vs. Linear Thinking: Understanding the Key Differences, showcases how linear thinking can be applied, stating that linear thinking “simplifies complex situations by breaking them down into smaller, more manageable tasks, focusing on cause-and-effect relationships”. (Critical Thinking secrets, 2026). On the other hand, the article also states that “Systems thinking emphasizes the interrelationships and patterns between system elements… offers a comprehensive way of understanding complex systems, as it focuses on the relationships between system components rather than individual components”. System thinking is so important to system engineering because of the complexity systems and their elements. The number of variables that are present in system engineering call for a more holistic approach, and that holistic approach requires that system engineers look at every element to build a system that is within a scope, functions properly, performs the functions it is called to do, and satisfies what stakeholders want from the system.

Using Model-Based System Engineering

When it comes to a model-based approach, and why its applicable in the design of complex digital systems, its best to understand why those systems are complex and how the model-based approach can be used in system engineering. As mentioned above, systems are made up of different elements, and they can grow to be more complex as the scope is identified and established. Complexity can come in two forms, those being essential and accidental complexity. Essential complexity is the natural level of complexity that is inherited in the system. In other words, as the scope grows, a natural level of complexity grows as well, and it cannot be lowered, but managed and controlled as long as its identified. Accidental complexity is not natural and is often applied by inefficiencies in things like staff, processes, and tools. The model-based approach or MBSE is an approach to systems engineering that “uses models, not just documents, to capture requirements, design, analysis, and verification of systems” (reforge learning, 2026). Instead of reading through pages, diagrams and simulation models are used to represent the system’s behavior and structure. MBSE is applicable to systems engineering because of the increasing level of complexity that systems have in the current day and age. Where technology is constantly advancing, systems-of-systems are more common, and by modeling a system digitally, engineers can get a better understanding of the interactions and behavior of a system. MBSE also helps in enhancing communication and collaboration via visual representation and can help get stakeholders on the same page as engineers. It can also improve quality as it can help in reducing risk of errors and inconsistencies, as well as improving efficiency and speed by allowing early detection and correction issues. MBSE is also scalable to projects of various sizes and complexities and can aid in the lifecycle of a product. Some of the tools that are used in the MBSE approach are systems modeling language (SysML), software tools, simulation and analysis tools, management tools and more depending on the project. SysML is a graphical modeling language that extends unified modeling language and provides a standard to presenting systems with various diagrams to aid in visualization. Software tools serve to offer environments for creating, editing, and managing SysML models. The simulation and analysis tools work to help in testing systems and analyzing system behaviors under certain conditions. Finally, there are the management tools, as they aid in keeping track of the project and making sure the requirements are all tracked and compatible with the system design (IBM, 2026). Overall, the use of MBSE is essential in todays day and age, simply because of the increasing level of complexity systems have. Having systems-of-systems is more prevalent than ever before and having the ability to create models to showcase how systems behave and interact with system elements makes developing them and understanding them much easier
