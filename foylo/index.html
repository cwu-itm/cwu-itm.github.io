Systems Thinking and Why it Matters

Before learning about systems thinking, it was easy for me to think about a system as a group of separate parts. For example, when I think about an IT system, I might normally think about computers, software, networks, databases, and users as different things. Systems thinking takes a different approach. Instead of looking at each part by itself, it focuses on how those parts are connected and how they affect each other. This makes a lot of sense when looking at technology because problems usually do not stay in one area. A change to one part of an IT system can affect several other parts. For example, a company might install a new software application because it seems like a good solution to a business problem. The application itself might work perfectly, but it could still create problems if it does not work with the company’s existing systems. Employees might also need training, the IT department might need to change how the system is supported, and the security team might have new concerns about how information is stored.

This is one of the main reasons I think systems thinking is useful. It encourages people to look at the bigger picture before deciding that they have found the solution. Southern New Hampshire University explains systems thinking as a way of looking at how different parts of a system work together rather than treating each part as completely separate (Southern New Hampshire University, n.d.). In an IT environment, that kind of thinking can help prevent problems that might not be obvious at first

Looking at the System as a Whole

One of the main ideas I took from Jon Holt’s discussion of Systems Engineering is that the individual parts of a system do not always tell us everything we need to know. The relationships between those parts are also important. Holt explains that systems are better understood by looking at their components in the context of their relationships with other components and systems (Holt, n.d.). I think this is especially relevant to IT because almost everything is connected in some way. A business might have a database that connects to an application, which connects to a network, which connects employees to the application. There may also be cloud services, security systems, and outside vendors involved. If something changes in one area, there is a chance that another area will be affected.

A good example would be a company replacing its employee management software. At first, the project might seem simple: choose the new software, install it, and teach employees how to use it. However, there is a lot more to consider. The new system may need to connect to payroll. Human resources may need certain information from it. Managers may need access to employee reports. Employees may need to update their own information. IT has to maintain the system, and the security team has to make sure sensitive information is protected.

If the company only focuses on installing the software, it could miss some of these connections. The software could technically work while the overall project still causes problems. Systems thinking helps prevent that kind of narrow thinking by encouraging people to consider the entire system and the relationships within it.

Complexity in Systems Engineering

Another idea that stood out to me from Holt’s book is the role of complexity. Modern systems can become complicated very quickly, especially when more components and relationships are added. Holt discusses complexity as one of the major challenges in Systems Engineering and also connects it with communication and understanding (Holt, n.d.).

Another idea that stood out to me from Holt’s book is the role of complexity. Modern systems can become complicated very quickly, especially when more components and relationships are added. Holt discusses complexity as one of the major challenges in Systems Engineering and also connects it with communication and understanding (Holt, n.d.).

Not all complexity comes from the same place. Some complexity is simply part of the system. A large company, for example, may naturally have many employees, departments, applications, databases, and business processes. It would be unrealistic to expect that system to be simple. However, organizations can also create unnecessary complexity through poor processes, disconnected systems, or unclear communication.

This is where systems thinking can be helpful. Instead of seeing complexity as one giant problem, a team can start breaking down the relationships that are creating the problem. They can ask questions such as: What depends on this system? Who uses it? What information goes into it? Where does that information go afterward? What could happen if something changes? These questions may seem simple, but they can reveal problems that might otherwise be overlooked. The Systems Thinker also emphasizes looking at relationships and patterns rather than only focusing on individual problems (The Systems Thinker, n.d.). That approach can be useful when a problem keeps coming back even after people have tried to fix it.

The Importance of Communication

Systems are not only difficult because of technology. People are another major part of the equation. Different people involved in a project can have completely different ideas about what the system should do. This can make communication a major challenge.

For example, a software developer might be focused on making sure an application works correctly. A manager might be more concerned with the budget and whether the project will be completed on time. A customer might care about whether the application is easy to use. A security professional might be focused on protecting the company’s information. All of these concerns are valid, but they are not the same.

Holt discusses complexity, communication, and understanding as closely connected problems in Systems Engineering (Holt, n.d.). That connection makes sense because a complicated system is harder to explain, and poor communication can make it even harder for people to understand what is actually happening.

This is one area where systems thinking can change the way a team approaches a project. Instead of assuming that everyone sees the system the same way, the team can recognize that each stakeholder has a different perspective. Understanding those perspectives can help the team communicate more clearly and make better decisions.

What Is Model-Based Systems Engineering?

Systems thinking helps us understand a system, but there is still a question of how to communicate all of that information. This is where Model-Based Systems Engineering, or MBSE, comes in.

Holt explains that traditional Systems Engineering relies heavily on documents to hold information about a system. MBSE moves more of that information into a model that represents the system (Holt, n.d.). The model is not supposed to contain every single detail. Instead, it is an abstraction that contains the information that is useful for understanding and developing the system.

I think this idea is easier to understand when thinking about a map. A map does not show every single detail that exists in the real world. It leaves out things that are not important for the purpose of the map. If you are using a map to find a restaurant, you probably do not need to know every tree or building along the road. You need the information that helps you get where you are going.

An MBSE model works in a similar way. It provides a representation of the system that focuses on the information people actually need. This can make a complicated system easier to understand without having to read through a huge collection of separate documents.

Models and Different Views

> One thing I found interesting about MBSE is the idea of having different views of the same model. Different stakeholders do not necessarily need the same information. A developer may need technical information, while an executive may be more interested in cost, risk, and overall project status.

> Holt explains that views should have a particular stakeholder, purpose, and set of relevant information. The different views also need to remain consistent with each other (Holt, n.d.). This is important because simply having a bunch of diagrams does not automatically mean an organization has a useful model.

For example, imagine a company has one diagram showing how information moves through an application and another showing the different components of the system. If the two diagrams contradict each other, they are not helping the team very much. A useful model should keep those different views connected so that everyone is working from the same understanding of the system.

This is where the idea of a model acting as a type of “single source of truth” becomes useful. Instead of different teams keeping their own versions of information, the model can provide a shared representation of the system. That could reduce confusion when changes are made because the team has a central place to understand how those changes affect the rest of the system.

Structure and Behavior

Holt also explains that a system model can be looked at from two important perspectives: structure and behavior. Structure is basically about what the system is made of and how the different elements are connected. Behavior is about how the system works, including the order of events and the conditions under which something happens (Holt, n.d.).

Both are important. Knowing what components are in a system does not necessarily explain what the system actually does. Going back to the online shopping example, the structure might include customers, a website, a payment system, an inventory database, and a shipping service. The behavior would explain what happens when a customer places an order. The system needs to process the payment, update inventory, create the order, and eventually send the information to shipping.

Looking at both structure and behavior gives the team a better understanding of how the system actually operates. It can also make it easier to identify what might happen if one part of the system changes.

Applying Systems Thinking and MBSE to IT

> I can see systems thinking being useful in almost every area of IT. IT projects rarely involve one piece of technology by itself. There are usually people, processes, hardware, software, security requirements, and business goals involved.

Consider a company moving some of its systems to the cloud. It would be easy to focus only on the technical side of the migration. However, the company also needs to think about employees, security, costs, data, existing applications, compliance requirements, and how the change will affect customers.

A systems-thinking approach would encourage the project team to look at all of these connections before making the change. MBSE could then help represent those relationships in a model. The team could use a variety of views to show technical components, system relationships, requirements, and other information that different stakeholders need.

This could be especially useful when a project becomes complicated. Instead of relying on one person to remember how everything fits together, the model can provide a shared representation of the system. It can also give the team a better chance of identifying problems before they become expensive to fix.

Some Challenges With MBSE

Even though MBSE has some clear advantages, I do not think it should be treated as a magic solution. Creating a useful model takes time, and people need to understand how to use it. If a model is not maintained, it can become outdated and stop representing the actual system.

Another challenge is that a model is still a simplified version of reality. It cannot contain every possible detail. Holt points out that models can leave out relevant information because they are abstractions of the real system (Holt, n.d.). This means that teams still need to think carefully about what information belongs in the model and make sure the model serves its intended purpose.

There can also be resistance to changing the way people work. Someone who has spent years using traditional documents may not immediately see why a model-based approach is better. Organizations may need to provide training and give people time to become comfortable with new processes and tools.

Conclusion

After looking at systems thinking and MBSE, I see them as two ideas that work well together. Systems thinking is more about the way we approach a problem. It encourages us to step back, look at the bigger picture, and understand how different parts of a system affect one another. MBSE provides a more structured way to capture and communicate that understanding.

The biggest takeaway for me is that an IT system is rarely just about technology. There are people, processes, requirements, business goals, and other systems involved. Ignoring one of those areas can create problems somewhere else. Looking at the whole system gives teams a better chance of understanding those connections.

Overall, systems thinking gives us a better way to approach complex problems, while MBSE gives us a way to organize and communicate what we learn about those problems. For people working in IT, I think both ideas are valuable because technology continues to become more connected and complicated. Being able to understand those connections, communicate them clearly, and consider the effects of changes before they happen can make a big difference in whether a technology project succeeds.
